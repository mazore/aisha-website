A talking textbook for healthcare workers with no internet, no power, and no time to lose.

AISHA is an offline, battery-powered voice assistant that answers clinical questions in Krio — built for health workers in low-resource clinics across Sierra Leone.

Read the publication See pilot results
The Problem

Medical knowledge doesn't reach the workers who need it most

Sierra Leone has a fraction of the healthcare workforce of high-income countries, and many frontline workers practice with limited formal training and no reliable way to check clinical guidance in the moment.

26× fewer healthcare workers per capita than high-income countries
82% of clinics in the region have no internet access
  • Language gap. Official medical guidelines are written in English, but health workers and patients communicate more naturally in Krio.
  • Connectivity gap. Cloud-based tools like smart speakers or chatbots simply don't work where most clinics operate — offline, off-grid, and far from reliable power.
  • Information gap. Without a working reference on hand, healthcare workers fall back on memory and training that may be years out of date.
The Solution

A device that works where the internet doesn't

AISHA is a local information-retrieval system, not a cloud-based generative chatbot — it answers from a preloaded, fact-checked knowledge base, which keeps it working offline and reduces the risk of hallucinated medical advice.

  • Fully offline. Runs entirely on-device — no internet or cell connection required, anywhere in the clinic.
  • Speaks Krio. A custom-trained voice model built from the ground up on collected Krio speech data, so the interface matches how health workers actually talk.
  • Grounded answers. Draws on guidelines, protocols, and materials from global health organizations, hospitals, and ministries of health — not open-ended generation.
  • Built for the clinic. Rechargeable battery, durable enclosure, and a louder speaker added after direct feedback from the field.
  • Two modes. Instant answers on demand, like a spoken encyclopedia, or guided learning modules for practicing clinical decision-making.
Impact

From prototype to pilot, in real clinics

AISHA has been piloted across clinics and Peripheral Health Units in Makeni, Sierra Leone, in partnership with Sierra Leone's Ministry of Health and Sanitation and local hospital staff.

8clinics piloted
400+participants in Krio voice data collection
70 hrsof Krio speech collected, 35% human-reviewed via our WhatsApp platform
10healthcare workers in pre/post clinical evaluation

Pilot evaluation used pre/post paper tests with healthcare workers, walking through realistic patient scenarios — including diabetic emergencies and hyperosmolar hyperglycemic state (HHS) diagnosis — before and after a short AISHA learning module.

These are results from an early-stage pilot evaluation — realistic clinical scenarios assessed on paper, not a large-scale clinical trial measuring real patient outcomes. We're continuing to expand and formalize this evaluation as AISHA scales.
